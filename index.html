Attempts to control communication in South Korea

When ballots ran out

On June 3, 2026, ballot shortages disrupted voting at polling stations across South Korea. The National Election Commission later reported shortages at 50 polling stations, with voting delayed or suspended at 22 locations.

In Songpa-gu, Seoul, some voters waited for hours as polling stations ran out of ballot papers.

Public frustration continued beyond the polling station. The event did not end when voting stopped.

A voting failure became a communication crisis. The shortage raised questions not only about election management, but also about trust, visibility, and how institutions communicate when a public system fails.

Section 2 · Ballots ran out

At Jamsil 7-dong Polling Station No. 2, voting was interrupted because ballots ran out. Voting eventually continued until 10 p.m., four hours after the official closing time.

Some voters left after waiting for hours. Others remained and protested, arguing that their right to vote had been compromised.

The shortage itself was an election-management failure.
